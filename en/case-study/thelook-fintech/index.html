The health of a loan portfolio, from BigQuery to Looker

TheLook Fintech is a fictional fintech that lends to independent online stores so they can buy inventory. Treasury needed to know, quickly and without asking for reports, whether they were lending more than was prudent.

In 20 seconds

  • I processed 270,299 loans ($4.17B) in BigQuery into tables ready for Treasury, one of them connected to Google Sheets.
  • I built a self-service Looker dashboard that turns the outstanding balance red when it breaks the limit: today, $3.08B against $3.0B.
  • Recommendation: slow new lending, watch concentration in 4 states and review the risk of debt-consolidation loans (56% of the total).

01The problem

The company is in its growth stage and wants to scale fast. In a meeting with Trevor, head of Treasury, these questions came up, each with its metric:

QuestionMetricWhy it matters
How do we watch cash flow?Amount lent per yearCash out must not exceed cash in.
Why do customers borrow?Purpose (application.purpose)Purpose is strongly linked to the likelihood of repayment.
Where are the borrowers?Location (state → region)Geographic concentration raises the risk of collective defaults.
How much is outstanding?Outstanding balance vs $3B limitIt is the most the team considers prudent to lend.
Which part is going wrong?% of balance by loan statusLate, charged-off and defaulted loans are the real risk.

02The data

The fintech BigQuery dataset, with two tables and an external CSV:

  • loan: 270,299 loans from 2012 to 2019 with amount, interest rate, issue date and year, loan status, state and a nested application record holding the purpose.
  • customers: annual income, home ownership, employment length and state of residence.
  • A CSV in Cloud Storage with the new classification of states into regions and subregions.

Limitations: the data is fictional (Google Cloud Training), stops in 2019 and has no repayments, so cash flow can only be seen from the outflow side.

03Process in BigQuery

Capstone part 1: collect, process and store. The SQL is what I ran in the lab.

Import the state classification from Cloud Storage

Trevor gave us a CSV mapping each state to a region and subregion. I loaded it as a standard table with LOAD DATA OVERWRITE and an explicit schema, using a wildcard to read every file.

LOAD DATA OVERWRITE fintech.state_region
(
  state string,
  subregion string,
  region string
)
FROM FILES (
  format = 'CSV',
  uris = ['gs://sureskills-lab-dev/future-workforce/da-capstone/temp_35_us/state_region_mapping/state_region_*.csv']);

Join on the right key and save the report

The report needed loan_id, loan_amount and the new region. There were two candidate queries: joining on region looked plausible, but the key both tables share is state. I saved the result with CTAS and opened it in Connected Sheets so Treasury could filter it as a spreadsheet (about 270,000 rows).

Joining on region would have used the old classification (or none). The JOIN decides what the report says.

-- Query B: join on the key both tables share
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE fintech.loan_with_region AS
SELECT
  lo.loan_id,
  lo.loan_amount,
  sr.region
FROM fintech.loan lo
INNER JOIN fintech.state_region sr
  ON lo.state = sr.state;

Nested data and deduplication

The loan purpose lives inside the nested application record. SELECT purpose fails; you need dot notation. With DISTINCT and CTAS I left a clean table of unique purposes.

-- purpose lives inside the nested RECORD `application`
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE fintech.loan_purposes AS
SELECT DISTINCT application.purpose AS purpose
FROM fintech.loan;

Cash-flow report by year

For the cash-flow question Trevor wanted one row per year. GROUP BY with COUNT and SUM, saved as a table for reuse.

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE fintech.loan_count_by_year AS
SELECT issue_year, COUNT(loan_id) AS loan_count
FROM fintech.loan
GROUP BY issue_year;

-- Cash out per year (Treasury's cash-flow question)
SELECT issue_year, SUM(loan_amount) AS total_amount
FROM fintech.loan
GROUP BY issue_year
ORDER BY issue_year;

Inside the metric

The main tile uses the LookML measure Outstanding Loans Amount. This is the SQL Looker generates: it sums the amount of every loan whose status is not Fully Paid.

-- SQL that Looker generates for the LookML measure
-- "Outstanding Loans Amount" (single value tile)
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(CASE WHEN (loan.loan_status <> 'Fully Paid'
                               OR loan.loan_status IS NULL)
                         THEN loan.loan_amount ELSE NULL END), 0)
       AS loan_outstanding_loans_amount
FROM `cloud-training-demos.fintech.loan` AS loan
LIMIT 500;

06Recommendations

I closed it with a one-slide executive summary for leadership, with no technical jargon:

Lending under control: what Treasury needs to see

Prepared for: TheLook Fintech executive team

Overview

TheLook Fintech is growing fast and must make sure what it lends does not exceed what comes in. We brought loan data together in BigQuery and turned it into reports and a dashboard to track cash flow, purpose and location.

Objective

  • Goal 1: report of loans issued per year.
  • Goal 2: self-service dashboard on portfolio health.
  • Impact: decide on time, reduce risk and tailor the offer.

Results

  • Loans ×20 between 2012 and 2019 (2,594 → 51,737).
  • Outstanding: $3.08B, above the limit and flagged red.
  • 87.9% current; 4 states concentrate the risk.

Next steps

  • Slow new lending while the balance is over the limit and prioritise collections: it is $80.6M over today.
  • Watch concentration in California, Texas, New York and Florida (72,476 outstanding loans, more than the other six top-10 states combined).
  • Review risk by purpose: 80% of loans are for debt consolidation or credit cards.

Then

  • Add an automatic alert when the balance crosses the threshold, not just a colour.
  • Bring in repayments (cash in) to see the full cash-flow picture, not just the outflows.
  • Collect Treasury's feedback on the dashboard and iterate, and segment borrowers by behaviour (as in my RFM project).

07What I learned

  • 01
    The JOIN key is a business decisionTwo almost identical queries produced different reports. I now check which column the tables truly share before joining.
  • 02
    A metric is not a column'Outstanding balance' in the lab sums the original amount of loans not fully paid, not the remaining principal. In a real job I'd ask for the live balance and document it in LookML.
  • 03
    A visible threshold beats a tableConditional formatting turns Treasury's rule ($3B) into something you see in one second.
  • 04
    Refreshing has a costEach tile refreshes as fast as its data changes: performance and cost, not just freshness.
  • 05
    Write for the decision-makerThe executive summary fits on one slide: title, context, goal, results with numbers and a next step. No jargon.
